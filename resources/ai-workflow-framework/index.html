The Trigger, Action, Owner Framework: AI Workflows Your Team Can Actually Explain

The best automation is the one your team can explain in a sentence. If it takes five minutes and sounds like a diagram from NASA, it's too complicated. Here's the 3-part framework for building the kind of AI workflow that actually gets used.

Inside: the Trigger, Action, Owner framework, the one-sentence explain test, a fill-in template for your own workflow, and one filled-in example (a new lead arriving).

The Problem

Most AI workflows die because nobody can explain them

It usually starts small: one AI step that saves someone twenty minutes. Then a branch gets added for the edge case. Then a second tool, because the first one couldn't quite do everything. Then a third AI agent to handle what the first two miss. Six weeks later, nobody on the team can say what the automation actually does without pulling up a diagram.

That's the real failure mode. Not that the automation breaks, though it usually does, but that nobody trusts it enough to rely on it, and nobody can fix it when it goes wrong because nobody fully understands it anymore.

Here's a simple test: ask the person actually using the automation what it does. If the explanation takes five minutes and sounds like a diagram from NASA, it's been made too complicated. A workflow that works sounds like one sentence.

The Framework

The 3 parts every AI workflow needs, and nothing more to start

A good workflow sounds like this: "When a lead arrives, AI summarizes what they want, creates the CRM task, and the salesperson approves the first reply." That sentence has exactly three parts. Get those three right before you add anything else.

#PartWhat it means
1TriggerThe one specific event that starts the workflow. Not "when things come in" — the exact thing: a form submission, a DM, a missed call.
2ActionThe one thing AI does automatically. Not five things stitched together, one clear action with a clear result.
3OwnerThe specific person who checks the output before it's used, and what exactly they're checking for. Not "the system", a name.

The one-sentence test: ask the person who uses this workflow to explain what it does. If they can say it in one sentence, it's simple enough to trust. If it takes a diagram, simplify it before you build anything else on top.

Copy And Paste

The blank template

Prefer to paste straight into a doc? Here's the same three parts as one block you can copy and fill in directly.

Trigger, Action, Owner Template
WORKFLOW NAME: [name this workflow, e.g. "New lead follow-up"]

1. TRIGGER
This starts when: [the one specific event, not a vague category].

2. ACTION
AI automatically: [the one thing it does, and the result it produces].

3. OWNER
Before this is used, [name/role] checks: [exactly what they're checking, e.g. accuracy, tone, whether a price was mentioned].

Only after this runs cleanly for a while: consider adding a branch, another tool, or another AI step.
See It Filled In

Example: a new lead arrives

Here's the same template, filled in for the example from the video: a lead comes in and needs a fast, accurate first response.

1. Trigger

A new lead arrives, whether that's a form submission, a DM, or a missed call that gets logged.

2. Action

AI reads the lead's message, summarizes what they're actually asking for, and creates the CRM (customer relationship management) task automatically, so nothing sits unassigned.

3. Owner

The salesperson on duty reviews the summary and approves the first reply before it's sent. They're checking that the summary is accurate and that the reply matches how the business actually talks to leads.

Quick Start

Build your first workflow this week

1

Pick one recurring task

Something that already happens often enough to be worth automating, not a hypothetical.

2

Name the exact trigger

Not "when things come in", the one specific event: which form, which inbox, which channel.

3

Give AI exactly one action

Resist the urge to make it do three things at once. One action, one clear result.

4

Assign one human owner

A specific person, not "the team", who approves before anything goes out.

5

Run it for a week before adding anything

Only add branches, extra tools, or extra AI agents once the simple version has proven itself.

  • Could you explain this workflow to your team in one sentence?
  • Is there exactly one trigger, one action, and one owner, not several of each?
  • Does a named person approve it, not "the system"?
