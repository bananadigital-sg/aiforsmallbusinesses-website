The Business AI Prompt Framework

A beginner-friendly system for turning vague AI requests into useful, on-brand business output.

Inside: a five-part prompt framework, a fill-in business brief, one master prompt, and five ready-to-use prompts.

The Shift

Stop using AI like a search box

A search box gives you an answer. A capable assistant produces better work when it understands the business behind the request.

Why one-line prompts disappoint

  • They force AI to guess who your customer is.
  • They leave out your offer, proof, positioning, and brand voice.
  • They rarely define what a good result should look like.
  • They produce generic ideas that could belong to any business.

A stronger way to prompt

Before asking for the work, give AI a short business brief. Then define the job, show it what to use, and specify the format you want.

CONTEXT → TASK → USEFUL OUTPUT

You do not need a long prompt every time. You need the right information, organized clearly.

The Framework

Use the BRIEF method

Build every important business prompt from five ingredients. Use the full version for strategic work and a shorter version for everyday tasks.

LetterPrompt ingredientWhat to include
BBusiness backgroundWhat you do, what you sell, and what makes you different.
RReader or customerWho the output is for, what they want, and what they struggle with.
IIntended outcomeThe result you want AI to help create or improve.
EEvidence and examplesOffers, facts, proof, source material, and examples to follow.
FFormat and voiceChannel, length, structure, tone, constraints, and call to action.

The simple rule: if the output sounds generic, do not immediately ask for more ideas. Add missing context, show a better example, or tighten the format.

Copy And Paste

Your master business prompt

Replace the bracketed text. Keep this prompt as your reusable starting point for marketing, sales, planning, and customer communication.

Master Prompt
You are helping me as a practical business assistant.

BUSINESS BACKGROUND
My business is [what you do]. We sell [offer] and help [customer] achieve [result]. What makes us different is [difference].

READER OR CUSTOMER
This is for [specific audience]. They care about [desire] and are concerned about [problem or objection].

INTENDED OUTCOME
Create [specific deliverable] that helps me [business goal]. The reader should [desired next action].

EVIDENCE AND EXAMPLES
Use these facts, offers, examples, or source notes: [paste material]. Do not invent claims or details.

FORMAT AND VOICE
Use a [tone] voice. Make it [length and structure]. Include [required elements]. Avoid [phrases, claims, or styles].

Before drafting, list any critical information that is missing. If nothing critical is missing, produce the final answer.

Tip: save your completed business background and customer sections. Reuse them instead of rewriting them for every prompt.

Ready To Use

Five prompts for everyday business work

1Social content

Using my business brief, create 10 social post ideas for [audience]. Group them into education, trust, and offer-related posts. For each idea, include a hook, the core point, and a natural call to action. Avoid generic motivational advice.

2Email

Write a short email to [audience] about [topic or offer]. Open with a relevant problem, explain one useful idea, connect it to [offer], and end with [call to action]. Use my brand voice and do not invent urgency or results.

3Offer clarity

Review this offer description: [paste]. Identify what is unclear, generic, or unsupported. Then rewrite it so the customer, problem, outcome, deliverables, and next step are obvious. Keep all factual claims unchanged.

4Customer response

Draft a helpful reply to this customer message: [paste]. Acknowledge the concern, answer directly using these policies or facts: [paste], and suggest the most appropriate next step. Keep the tone calm and human.

5Weekly planning

Based on these priorities, deadlines, and constraints: [paste], create a realistic five-day plan. Separate must-do work from optional work, identify dependencies, and flag anything that cannot fit without moving another task.

Before And After

See what context changes

The vague request

Give me 10 Instagram post ideas.

Likely result: broad topics, recycled advice, and ideas that do not naturally connect to your offer.

The useful request

I run a bookkeeping service for independent tradespeople with teams of 1 to 10. They are good at their trade but often fall behind on receipts, cash-flow visibility, and tax preparation. Our offer is a fixed monthly bookkeeping package with plain-English reporting and a monthly review call. Create 10 Instagram post ideas that build trust with this audience. Group them into education, common mistakes, and behind-the-scenes process. For each, include a hook, one teaching point, and a soft call to action. Sound practical and reassuring, not corporate. Do not promise tax savings or guaranteed results.

Why it works

  • AI knows the business model and the offer.
  • The audience and their real problems are specific.
  • The desired result, structure, voice, and guardrails are clear.

Before you press send

  • Is every important fact accurate?
  • Does this sound like your business?
  • Would the intended customer understand it?
  • Is the next action clear?
