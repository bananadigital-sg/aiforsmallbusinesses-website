How to Get Your Business Listed in ChatGPT

OpenAI now lets businesses claim their listing inside ChatGPT through a tool called Business Hub. Here is who can do it, how to set it up step by step, and what it will and will not do for you.

The Short Version

What Business Hub is

On November 10, 2026, OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, and more of them now ask it to recommend a product, a service or a local business.

The tool for business owners is Business Hub (chatgpt.com/business-hub). It works a lot like a Google Business Profile: you claim your listing, correct the details, and see some basic numbers about how people find you.

Check First

Can you claim your business yet?

Maybe. It is a beta, and OpenAI says availability depends on your business's location and type. Here is where things stand:

  • Local businesses (shops, restaurants, salons, trades): OpenAI says local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing US listing. You claim a listing ChatGPT already has rather than creating one.
  • Ecommerce and online service businesses: can claim, and OpenAI's help article says this now includes businesses based outside the US.
  • Everyone: you must own the business or be authorized to represent it, and you need to pass verification.

The quickest way to find out is to open Business Hub and search for your business name. If it shows up, you can start a claim. If not, check again in a few weeks. OpenAI says it plans to expand to more markets and categories over time.

Before You Start

Have these ready

The setup takes about 15 minutes if you have everything on hand. It stalls if you don't.

  • A ChatGPT account that belongs to the business, ideally on your business email, not a personal login
  • Access to your business phone, business email or website domain settings (for the verification code)
  • A government-issued photo ID
  • Your business registration details, with your legal business name (OpenAI asks for your trade name in a separate field)
  • A short business description, your logo and a cover photo
  • Your delivery and return policies, if you sell products
Step By Step

How to claim your business in ChatGPT

These steps follow OpenAI's help article. The screens may change during the beta, so check that page if something looks different.

1

Sign in with your business account

Whoever signs in becomes the administrator of the listing, so use an account the business controls.

2

Open Business Hub and search

Find Business Hub in the ChatGPT sidebar or go to chatgpt.com/business-hub. Type your business name.

3

Select your listing

Pick the match and confirm which ChatGPT account will manage it.

4

Verify your connection to the business

Choose a verification method. The options vary by listing (see below).

5

Wait for approval

OpenAI emails you a decision or asks for more information. Submitting a claim does not give you access until it is approved.

6

Complete the identity check

After approval, verify your identity with Persona, the service OpenAI uses for this. You need it before you can publish changes, view insights, connect a product feed or create an agent. Approval publishes a new profile automatically, so only continue when your details are ready.

7

Update your details

Edit your website, description, category, logo, cover photo and policies. OpenAI reviews changes before publishing them, so updates can take time to appear.

The verification options

OpenAI lists these, and you will only see the ones available for your listing:

  • Business phone: you get a code by text or automated call.
  • Business email: you get a code at your business email address.
  • Website domain: you add a short text record to your domain's DNS settings (the behind-the-scenes settings for your website address). Your web person can do this in a few minutes.
  • Shopify: you connect your Shopify store.

Two checks, two jobs. The claim verification proves you are connected to the business. The Persona check proves who you are. You need both, and the blue verified check only appears after Persona approves you.

Once You're In

What you can manage

According to OpenAI, a verified business can update:

  • Website, business description and category
  • Logo and cover photo
  • Delivery policy and return policy
  • Prices and availability, through a product feed: upload a CSV file, enter a hosted feed URL, or connect Shopify

You can also add teammates or an agency from Business Hub. Each person needs their own ChatGPT account, and personal invitations only work for the exact email address they were sent to, so don't forward them.

What the insights show

The insights cover the past 28 days:

  • Mentions: for an online business, a mention counts when ChatGPT references your website (your brand name alone does not count). For a local business, it counts when ChatGPT shows a result for your location. Treat this as an impression, not a sale.
  • Website clicks: how often people clicked through to your site.
  • Discovery topics: broad themes in answers where your business came up.

Numbers only appear once there is enough activity to meet OpenAI's privacy thresholds, so a blank does not always mean zero. You will not see anyone's questions or conversations. OpenAI says businesses do not receive individual prompts or customer chats.

Optional

Business Agents

A Business Agent answers customer questions inside ChatGPT using the information you provide. A customer who finds your business can open a clearly labeled chat with your agent, separate from their main conversation, and ask things like:

  • "What's the difference between these two products?"
  • "Which one suits a beginner?"
  • "What's your return policy?"

Know the limits before you build one. The agent explains products and policies and links to your website, where customers buy or book. It does not take payments or bookings itself. To publish an agent you need an approved claim, Persona approval, a website, and an eligible ecommerce or online service business. You preview it with sample questions first, and nothing goes live until you click Publish.

Be Realistic

What claiming will not do

Claiming your listing will not move you up the list. OpenAI's help article says claiming "doesn't guarantee increased visibility or give you direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations." It also says advertising does not influence ChatGPT's independent answers.

What claiming does do is put you in charge of the facts. When ChatGPT describes your business, the website, hours, policies and description come from you rather than from an old directory listing.

OpenAI also says ChatGPT may use other sources when it answers questions about your business, and it doesn't name them. So keep your website, your Google Business Profile and other listings accurate and consistent. That's the part you can control.

Copy And Paste

Two prompts to get your listing ready

Paste these into ChatGPT or Claude before you fill in your profile.

Write your business description
Write a business description for my ChatGPT Business Hub profile.

Business name: [name]
What we sell or do: [products or services]
Who our customers are: [describe them]
Where we operate: [city, region, or online]
What makes us different: [2 or 3 real reasons]

Keep it under 120 words, plain English, no hype words like "best" or "leading". Write it so a customer instantly knows what we do, who it is for and where we are.
Turn customer questions into clear answers
Here are the questions customers ask us most often, by email, phone, DM and in person:
[paste your list]

For each one, write a short, factual answer in plain English. Flag any answer where you need information from me instead of guessing. Then tell me which answers belong on my website's FAQ page and which belong in my policies.

Clear answers on your own website help customers whether they find you through ChatGPT, Google or a friend.

Avoid These

Five common mistakes

  • Claiming with a personal account. If that person leaves or loses access, getting the listing back is a headache.
  • Rushing the Persona step. Approval publishes your profile automatically. Have your description, logo and photo ready first.
  • Names that don't match. Use your legal name exactly as it appears on your registration documents, and put your trade name in its own field.
  • Ignoring a listing someone else claimed. Use Dispute ownership from the three-dot menu on the profile. If it says Contact support, the listing is locked and you need OpenAI support.
  • Paying anyone who promises a top spot in ChatGPT. OpenAI itself says claiming does not guarantee visibility.
Cost

Does it cost anything?

OpenAI's announcement and help article don't list a fee for claiming. The help article says you don't need an Ads account or a ChatGPT Enterprise agreement to claim an eligible business. Since this is a beta, check Business Hub for the current terms.

Claim it, verify it, keep it accurate. It won't buy you a better spot, but it means ChatGPT gets your details from you.
Questions

Common questions

What is ChatGPT Business Hub?

OpenAI's tool at chatgpt.com/business-hub where an eligible business can claim its listing in ChatGPT, manage its details and see aggregated insights. It launched in beta on November 10, 2026.

Can businesses outside the US use it?

Ecommerce and online service businesses outside the US can now claim and publish agents, according to OpenAI's help article. Local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing US listing.

Can I see what people asked ChatGPT about my business?

No. You see aggregated topics and counts, never individual questions or conversations.

Can I delete my profile later?

Only unpublished drafts can be deleted. OpenAI's help article says its support team cannot delete, unpublish or hide a published profile.

I have more than one brand. Do I need separate profiles?

Yes. Each brand needs its own profile, and locations are not claimed automatically.

Checked against OpenAI's announcement and help article on November 24, 2026. Business Hub is in beta and details may change.
